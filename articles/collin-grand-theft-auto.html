Collin Imagines the Drive Is Grand Theft Auto

The car is his. The mission is a grocery run. He is narrating it anyway.

By Wendell Pike, Sports & Games Desk · November 24, 2026 · 2 min read

Collin drove his own car on Monday and imagined, the entire way, that he was playing Grand Theft Auto. Nobody assigned him the mission. The radio was not a headset. The only open world was the one with a speed limit and a grocery list.

Sports & Games Desk reporter Wendell Pike, who has covered video games since cartridges and has strong opinions about shells, rode along in spirit, which is to say he heard about it afterward, in detail, from Collin. "He kept a commentary going," Pike said. "Out loud. To the windshield. A minivan changed lanes and he called it a rival crew."

A yellow light is not a wanted level. We have explained this. He heard a soundtrack anyway.

Wendell Pike, Sports & Games Desk

The mission log, as narrated

  • Objective. Reach the store. He described this as "extraction" and also as "we need milk."
  • The mirror. Promoted, by him, from a way to see traffic into a mission marker.
  • Wanted level. One star, self-awarded, for rolling a yellow. The star was imaginary. The light was not.
  • The getaway. He parked between the lines, turned the key, and told the empty seat the job was clean.

The car, for the record, is his. The keys were in his pocket when he left. The only thing stolen was the tone of a video game, applied to a drive so ordinary that a dune and a side mirror did most of the set design.

Asked what the high score was, Collin said he had "made every light," then corrected himself, then said the correction did not count because it happened after the cutscene. Pike noted that Grand Theft Auto has consequences, and so does a real road, and that Collin seemed interested in only one of those two facts.

At press time he was back in the driveway, engine off, still slightly in the game. The milk had made it home. The wanted level had not.
