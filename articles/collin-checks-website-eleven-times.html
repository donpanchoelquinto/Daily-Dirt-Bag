Collin Reportedly Refreshing Website to See If He Is on It. He Is.

Eleven visits before lunch and zero acts of self-awareness, according to the analytics desk.

By Beatrice Okafor, Editor-in-Chief · November 20, 2026 · 2 min read

The Daily Dirt Bag's analytics dashboard, a spreadsheet maintained by a single tired intern named Gerald, recorded eleven separate visits from one device before noon on Thursday. The device has been positively identified as belonging to Collin, who browses in a manner our data team describes as "aggressively casual."

"He loads the page, scrolls straight past the news, and checks whether his name is on it," said Editor-in-Chief Beatrice Okafor. "Then he closes the tab, waits four minutes, and does it again. That is not a pattern. That is a ritual."

Gerald's spreadsheet, which has three tabs and one pie chart, shows that Collin's traffic spikes at predictable times: right after lunch, right after dinner, and, inexplicably, at 2:14 a.m. "He's either very dedicated or has a very flexible sleep schedule," Gerald said. "I'm choosing not to find out."

Reached for comment, Collin said he was "just making sure the formatting looks okay," a phrase he has now used on three separate occasions, including once at a restaurant, about the menu.

If you truly did not care, you would not know the page has a News tab. He knows the News tab. He has opinions about the News tab.

Hollis Granger, Culture Desk

A brief timeline of Thursday

  • 8:02 a.m. First visit. Duration: nine seconds. Scrolled directly to the bottom.
  • 9:47 a.m. Second visit, immediately after telling a coworker he "hadn't even looked."
  • 10:31 a.m. Visits four through six, in rapid succession, on what appears to be a different browser.
  • 11:58 a.m. Visit eleven. Lingered on his own headline for a full forty seconds.

The paper's legal desk asked that we clarify: refreshing a website about yourself is not a crime, and is in fact extremely normal. "We have all searched our own names," said General Counsel Ingrid Solheim. "Most of us just have the decency to be quiet about it."

At press time the page had been refreshed again. The Daily Dirt Bag would like to remind the subject that he is welcome to leave a comment below, that we will read it, and that he will be mentioned in our response.
