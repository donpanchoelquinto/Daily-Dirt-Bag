Dirt Bag Launch Postponed Again; Sources Blame Liability and Moral Objections

A stack of letters, a shortage of lawyers, and one very calm subject.

By Dale Overholt, Investigations Editor · May 7, 2025 · 2 min read

The Daily Dirt Bag announced Tuesday that its long-awaited full launch has been postponed for a second time, citing what editors called "logistical issues, liability concerns, and moral objections." The announcement was delivered on letterhead that our own staff had to print, because the printer was also, according to a memo, "under review."

Fourteen letters from a group calling itself the Department of Common Decency arrived at the newsroom over a single weekend. Investigations Editor Dale Overholt examined all of them and noted that every one was written in what he described as "suspiciously familiar handwriting."

We are not saying the letters are fake. We are saying that nobody has ever met the Department.

Dale Overholt, Investigations Editor

General Counsel Ingrid Solheim, a title she granted herself in 2023, advised that the paper should proceed "with caution, and possibly a lawyer." When asked whether a lawyer had been consulted, Solheim confirmed that she had called one, left a voicemail, and was still waiting.

The decision, editors stressed, was not made lightly. It was made over two meetings, one lunch, and a group chat that Overholt described as "mostly gifs." A motion to launch anyway "and see what happens" was voted down five to one, with the lone dissenter identified only as "a guy who really wanted it out by Friday."

Collin, the paper's most frequently discussed subject, called the delay "a win for everyone" in a statement released through a friend. He then asked, in a follow-up message, who exactly the letters were from, and whether he could see them.

"That is the most telling part," said Overholt. "He wasn't relieved. He was curious. A man at peace does not ask for the paperwork."

Sources inside the newsroom say the delay has given staff time to improve the site, fix several typos, and sit through a mandatory ninety-minute seminar titled "Ethics: A Beginner's Guide." Attendance was high. Comprehension, according to the instructor, was "a work in progress."

The Daily Dirt Bag expects to relaunch once its moral objections have been resolved, or at minimum rebranded. Readers who wish to be notified may use the Sign up button at the top of the page.
