Study Finds Collin's "Two Minutes Away" Is Scientifically Closer to Twenty

Fourteen texts, one stopwatch, and a margin of error the size of a lunch break.

By Priya Venkataraman, Science & Punctuality Desk · July 8, 2023 · 2 min read

In a first-of-its-kind study, the Daily Dirt Bag's Science & Punctuality Desk has confirmed what friends, family, and at least one patient waiter have long suspected: when Collin says he is "two minutes away," the true figure is closer to twenty.

Researchers logged fourteen separate "almost there" messages over a three-month period, timing each from the moment of the text to the moment of an actual, physical arrival. The mean gap was eighteen minutes, with a median of seventeen and a single outlier, referred to in the paper as "the Costco incident," of fifty-one.

The paper's methodology was rigorous by the standards of a newsroom with one stopwatch. Each message was screenshotted, time-stamped, and filed in a binder labeled "Evidence," which Venkataraman admits she purchased specifically for the occasion and now keeps on her desk "as a reminder."

"We initially assumed our equipment was faulty," said lead researcher Priya Venkataraman. "We replaced the stopwatch twice. Then we replaced the intern. The numbers did not change."

He does not lie about the time. He lives in a different time zone, one that is slower and more optimistic.

Priya Venkataraman, Science & Punctuality Desk

Outside experts were consulted, in the sense that Venkataraman called her uncle, who is a pilot. "In my line of work, 'two minutes' means two minutes," he said. "If you say two minutes and it's twenty, people stop trusting you, and also there is an incident report."

The authors invited Collin to review the findings prior to publication. At last check he had replied with the words "be there in two minutes." The study's authors have since stopped waiting.
