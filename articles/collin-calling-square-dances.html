Collin Takes Up Calling, and Yes, He Is Calling Everyone

He has a hat, a tie, and a microphone he does not need. The dancers have a choice. Nobody has used it.

By Hollis Granger, Culture Desk · November 26, 2026 · 2 min read

Collin has taken up calling. In square-dance terms, that means he stands in the middle of the floor and tells everyone where to go next, in a voice that carries, while the dancers do the walking. He has the hat. He has the tie. He has, according to a source at the venue, not yet learned the steps, which is considered a minor detail among callers.

"Allemande left," Collin told a party of eleven at a rented hall on Saturday. Asked what an allemande left was, he said it was "the one where you go left, and then everybody sort of re-finds their people." Two dancers went left. Nine re-found their people, who had been standing next to them the whole time.

A caller's first job is to get the room moving. Collin's first job was to make sure the room knew he was the one talking.

Hollis Granger, Culture Desk

Calls issued this season

  • "Swing your partner." He swung himself, twice, and then looked for someone to take the credit.
  • "Promenade home." Interpreted by the room as an instruction to find the exit. Several did.
  • "Everybody do-si-do." He did a very confident do-si-do with a coat rack.
  • "Come on, people." Not a square-dance call. A personal request. Nobody came on.

Collin says he is calling people because "people need direction," a philosophy he has held since being a passenger in other people's cars. Friends report that he has also begun calling them, by phone, at odd hours, to announce where he is, which is usually closer than he thinks.

The Daily Dirt Bag asked for a comment from the dancers. One guest, who had been on the floor for the entire set and asked not to be named, said, "He is a very loud person with a very nice hat, and I would like him to know the hat is the best part." The hall has since asked Collin to call the next dance from the stage, where the microphone works.

At press time he was still calling. The floor was still reading his instructions with polite, patient confusion. Somewhere, a caller's manual is being opened for the first time, and it is the manual that is nervous.
