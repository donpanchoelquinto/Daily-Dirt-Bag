Collin and His Father Plan a Data Center in the Middle of the Snake River

They sat on the cliff and called it a site visit. The river was not on the email chain.

By Dale Overholt, Investigations Editor · November 26, 2026 · 2 min read

Collin and his father have selected a location for their data center. It is the middle of the Snake River. They announced this by sitting on a cliff above it and looking, for a long time, like men who had already won the argument with the water.

The pitch, delivered mostly by the father with his hands clasped, and received mostly by Collin with his back to the camera, is that the cooling problem is solved. "The river is right there," Collin later explained, pointing at the part of the plan that is a river. Servers, he said, run hot. Water, he said, is cold. The rest, he said, was "basically a foundation."

They have a view, a gesture, and no permit. In this family that counts as a term sheet.

Dale Overholt, Investigations Editor

Investigations Editor Dale Overholt obtained a one-page document titled "PHASE 1," written in the notes app and emailed to nobody. It lists the river, the highway on the far bank, and the word "power?" with the question mark left in.

What the plan currently includes

  • The building. To stand in the channel, "where the view is." Fish were not consulted and would like that noted.
  • Cooling. The Snake River, used as a heat sink, and also as the floor.
  • Access. A road that already exists on the bank, plus a bridge Collin described as "the state's problem, eventually."
  • Staffing. Two. One talks. One nods. Both are already on site, if a cliff counts.

A county clerk who was shown the overlook photo said she could not accept a river as a parcel number. Collin's father replied that the parcel was "the wet one in the middle," and asked whether the office took plans by pointing. The office does not.

Collin told the Daily Dirt Bag the project is still in due diligence, which in this case means they have not yet fallen in. He said the first racks could be "in the water by spring," a schedule the river has already met, having been in the water for some time. His father folded his hands again, which the minutes record as a yes.

At press time the Snake River was still a river. The data center remained a conversation between two men on a rock, which is the only part of the build that has so far stayed dry.
