Collin Inaugurated, Though Only in His Imagination

The boutonniere was real. The office was not. The crowd was him.

By Beatrice Okafor, Editor-in-Chief · November 26, 2026 · 2 min read

Collin was inaugurated on Thursday, in a ceremony attended by Collin and reviewed, favorably, by Collin. The Daily Dirt Bag can find no record of an office, a ballot, or a second guest. What we can find is a suit, a light blue tie, and a boutonniere that suggests someone nearby owns a garden and a great deal of patience.

Witnesses say he stood still long enough for the portrait, then nodded as if a band had started. There was no band. "You kind of had to be there," he told a friend later. The friend had been there. The friend had seen a man in a field having a private promotion.

He took the oath with his eyes open, which was bold, because he was also the one administering it.

Beatrice Okafor, Editor-in-Chief

Pressed on who, exactly, had inaugurated him, Collin said it was "more of a movement." The movement, our desk has learned, is an imaginary cult. It has a name he will not share "until the merch is ready," a meeting place that is whichever room he is already in, and a membership roll with one line on it.

The cult, as imagined

  • Founder. Collin. Also the treasurer, the choir, and the person who claps.
  • Sacred text. A notes-app entry titled "thoughts," last edited to add the word "legacy."
  • Weekly rite. He looks at the portrait and tells himself the mountains showed up for him.
  • Dissent. Not permitted, and also not possible. The dissenter would have to be someone else.

Editor-in-Chief Beatrice Okafor, who has covered several of Collin's private governments, said this one at least dressed for the job. "The others wore confidence and a group chat," she said. "This one rented a flower."

Collin has already asked to be addressed by a title. He has not picked one. Front-runners include "the inaugural," which is not a title, and "sir," which he tried once at a drive-through and did not get. The imaginary cult is expected to ratify whatever he lands on, since it meets inside his head and has never lost a vote.

At press time the portrait was doing very well on his phone, and the cult had adjourned so its only member could get something to eat.
